Summary & Analysis

1. Basic information

  1. Title: How Multi-Recipient KEMs can help the Deployment of Post-Quantum Cryptography. (source: uploaded PDF)
  2. Authors: Joël Alwen, Matthew Campagna, Dominik Hartmann, Shuichi Katsumata, Eike Kiltz, Jake Massimo, Marta Mularczyk, Guillermo Pascual-Perez, Thomas Prest, Peter Schwabe. (uploaded PDF)
  3. Year: 2023 (document and references). (uploaded PDF)
  4. Venue: Technical report / short research paper (see references for related proceedings CCS 2023). (uploaded PDF)

2. Introduction and motivation

The paper addresses the increased communication cost imposed by post-quantum KEMs and proposes multi-recipient KEMs (mKEMs) — in particular mKyber and amKyber — as practical constructions to reduce bandwidth and computation in group/broadcast settings. The motivation is rooted in the substantially larger public keys and ciphertexts of PQC KEMs compared to classical ECC mechanisms (e.g., Kyber ciphertexts >> ECDH public keys). (uploaded PDF)

3. Objective

Produce practical mKEM constructions based on Kyber, propose parameters, analyze security tradeoffs (including adaptive corruption issues), and demonstrate gains in applications such as MLS and broadcast synchronization of HSM fleets. (uploaded PDF)

4. Main idea & innovation

  1. Use a single multi-recipient ciphertext that can be decomposed into small per-recipient shares.
  2. Adapt Kyber's building blocks (MLWE/MLWR, FO transform) to the mKEM setting and introduce a decomposable FO transform to enable efficient per-recipient decapsulation.
  3. Provide amKyber to obtain IND-CCA security under adaptive corruptions at modest overhead.

(uploaded PDF)

5. Methods and implementation

The constructions are built on standard Kyber-style polynomial MLWE operations, but with the matrix A shared across recipients; encoding optimizations (shorter message, coefficient dropping, bit dropping) reduce per-recipient v_i sizes; the decomposable FO transform enables CCA security while keeping per-recipient ciphertexts small. amKyber uses a two-instance trick inspired by broadcast encryption to achieve adaptively secure mPKE → mKEM. (uploaded PDF)

6. Computational and operational complexity

Multi-recipient ciphertext size grows O(N · |v_i|) but amortized per-recipient bytes can be far smaller than N independent KEMs because of the shared u. For concrete Kyber512 vs mKyber512 parameters: per-recipient amortized ~48 B (mKyber) vs 768 B (Kyber) for large groups; amKyber doubles mKyber ciphertexts but remains substantially smaller than N×Kyber. See Table 1 in the paper for parameter values. (uploaded PDF)

7. Results and evaluation

  1. Bandwidth savings: asymptotic ~16× improvement; concrete scenario savings demonstrated for TreeKEM/MLS commits and broadcast.
  2. Security: mKyber achieves IND-CCA (non-adaptive) and amKyber provides IND-CCA with adaptive corruptions (at additional size cost).'
  3. Tradeoffs: presence of many MLWER samples necessitates careful parameter selection and ongoing cryptanalysis vigilance.

9. Conclusions & future research

The paper gives a clear roadmap to reduce PQC deployment costs in multi-recipient settings via mKyber/amKyber and shows concrete gains in MLS/broadcast scenarios. Future research should target adaptive-security proof techniques, more detailed cryptanalysis for MLWER in the many-sample regime, implementation studies, and standardization/verification adaptation from Kyber artifacts.
