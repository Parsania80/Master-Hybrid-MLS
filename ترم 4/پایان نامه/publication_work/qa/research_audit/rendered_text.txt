Research Audit for Adaptive Hybrid TreeKEM
Scientific evidence and submission decisions
Prepared 14 October 2026
This audit accompanies the English article Adaptive Arity and TreeCover Migration in Hybrid TreeKEM.
The defensible contribution is a policy for gradually changing tree arity while temporarily using old-tree
nodes to deliver secrets belonging to the new tree. The contribution is architectural and analytical. A
formal proof of the custom hybrid construction, an adaptive-corruption theorem, and a fully reproducible
evaluation are not established by the supplied materials.
The manuscript is suitable for author and supervisor scientific review. It is not yet a submission-ready
security specification. The most consequential outstanding decisions concern the exact cryptographic
profile, historical key knowledge during TreeCover retention, completeness of the evaluation, and the final
publication venue. The user directed that the thesis be authoritative, that Hamid Mala be included as
second author, and that no implementation information appear in the paper. This audit retains
implementation findings for internal review only.

2 Research extraction and evidence map
The research question is: how can a hybrid TreeKEM group increase arity while avoiding a complete
immediate rebuild of internal key state, and how should it decide when an old-tree delivery cover can be
retired? The significance is that expensive shared per-level material can make shallower trees attractive,
while sparse new trees and retained historical secrets create transition costs that steady-state comparisons
omit.
The proposed answer is to make the new root authoritative from the transition, retain eligible old nodes
only for delivery, populate the new tree through subsequent commits, and retire the old cover using
normalized endpoint costs. The core novelty should be claimed at this combination and lifecycle level.
Higher-arity TreeKEM, mKEM batching, hybrid KEMs, and post-quantum MLS are prior work.

3 Novelty assessment and closest work
Alwen et al., How Multi-Recipient KEMs can help the Deployment of Post-Quantum Cryptography,
explicitly discuss m-ary TreeKEM in Section 4, including a four-ary example. Their Table 1 supplies the
768-byte public key, 704-byte common ciphertext, and 48-byte recipient ciphertext parameters. Their
MLS treatment already explains why shared encapsulation material changes the depth/fanout tradeoff. The
thesis cannot claim these mechanisms as its inventions. The supplied 15-page paper was extracted and its
construction, parameter, security, and MLS sections inspected.
Chevalier, Lebrun, Martinelli, and Plût's The Art of Bonsai analyzes MLS tree evolution and degree
choice. The official ePrint record identifies publication at IEEE EuroS&P 2025 and reports that useful
degree depends on the encryption parameters. It is essential related work absent from the thesis
bibliography. Its full PDF could not be retrieved because the source returned access errors; the manuscript
confines its account to the official abstract and metadata. Detailed dominance or novelty claims against its
algorithms remain unresolved. The reference cites the verified ePrint record instead of guessing publisher
pagination or a DOI that could not be confirmed through the publisher.
The two IETF draft families address different questions. The hybrid-cipher-suite draft concerns cipher-
suite choices. The Amortized PQ MLS Combiner couples two MLS sessions to change the frequency of
costly post-quantum updates. They are relevant alternatives, but neither the presence of hybridization nor
amortized post-quantum cost alone distinguishes this thesis. Their dated draft status is recorded explicitly;
neither is presented as a standard.
X-Wing establishes concrete hybrid KEM results for its own selected components. KEM Combiners
supplies general motivation for construction-specific robust combination. The symbolic TreeKEM work
establishes proof results for a precise MLS specification. None supplies a proof for this custom combiner
plus arity transition plus retained cover. The paper uses these works to delimit what must be proved, not to
imply inherited security.
The recommended contribution claim is: “We investigate an adaptive-arity migration design for hybrid
TreeKEM that uses an authenticated old-tree cover to deliver new-tree path secrets during gradual
population, and analyze the resulting communication and retained-key tradeoffs.” This claim is supported
by the supplied design. A claim of being the first such construction would need a broader full-text
literature review and is deliberately not made.

4.3 Cover validity and retirement
Set coverage, disjointness, and current membership are necessary but insufficient for secure delivery. A
removed member may still know the private key of an old node whose current labels appear harmless. The
paper makes historical key knowledge an explicit eligibility obligation. A full algorithm must define key
generations, possession, invalidation, and allowed reuse in both structures; the thesis does not provide a
formal inductive invariant proving them correct.

4.4 Security claim repairs
The thesis's suggestion that an old compromised cover route delays recovery only in a local subtree is too
strong. A recovered path secret can propagate to the new root. If the attacker also possesses the required
prior key-schedule state, exposure can affect the group epoch secret. The paper explains this conditional
group-wide consequence and does not claim a finite recovery bound without a schedule that eliminates all
attacker-known delivery routes.
Forward secrecy is also conditional on key lifetimes and erasure. Retaining an old private key makes
recorded migration ciphertexts addressed to it potentially decryptable after later compromise. The exact
affected epochs depend on other key-schedule secrets. Deleting local retained keys cannot delete copies
already recorded by an attacker. The paper neither asserts a practical attack on a deployed product nor
repeats an unconditional preservation claim.
The mKyber source distinguishes its non-adaptive multi-user treatment from amKyber's adaptive-
corruption setting. This is a limitation of known proof techniques, not a demonstrated break. The thesis-
specific combiner requires its own argument. Classical authentication remains conceptually distinct from
post-quantum confidentiality; replacing a KEM does not make classical signatures quantum resistant.
5 Numerical audit
5.1 Recomputed analytical values
The key-and-KEM model is B = 800h + 704L + 80R, with R the sum of recipient endpoints over
core
nonempty levels. The healthy approximation uses rounded height h = ceil(log_d n), L = h, and R = (d −
1)h. The coefficients are therefore 1,584, 1,744, and 2,064 bytes per level for d = 2, 4, and 8. Every row in
manuscript Table 2 is recomputed from these definitions; none is described as measured wire traffic.
The model excludes per-recipient encrypted path secrets and authentication tags. A 32-byte path secret
plus a 16-byte tag would add 48R bytes before other framing, under the stated one-wrapper-per-recipient
assumption. The paper explicitly separates this accounting example from the authoritative but incomplete
cryptographic profile. Actual occupied sibling counts can differ from the full-capacity approximation,
especially at n just above a power boundary.
The five break-even examples were independently recalculated. Their first-cost formula is 1,504 times
target height plus 80(n − 1). The excess cost over one old-tree commit is divided by the future healthy per-
commit saving and rounded upward. The values 1, 11, 36, 73, and 147 are additional commits after the
transition commit. They assume immediate healthy operation afterward. Cover use can reduce the first
cost, while sparse later state can increase follow-up costs; the examples are neither a universal upper
bound nor a lower bound.
The occupancy expression 1 − (1 − s/n)^t assumes independent uniform updates in a fixed group.
Conditioning t on effective updates changes the sampling distribution. Removals and nonuniform update
rates violate the simple model. The paper retains the formula with these conditions but does not reproduce
numerical maturity forecasts without the exact subtree decomposition and sampling definition.

7 Manuscript organization and abstract review
The title names the mechanism and setting without promising proven post-quantum security. The abstract
states the transition problem, describes the single new-root authority and temporary old cover, gives one
analytical saving and one contrasting amortization example, and identifies the security limitation. It has no
citations. Its strongest result is the transparent communication tradeoff, not a claimed benchmark or a
theorem.
The manuscript progresses from introduction and related work to model and cryptographic specification,
migration, conditional security, mathematical communication analysis, discussion, and conclusion. This
order makes the mechanism understandable before assessing its security and cost. There is no empirical-
results section. The provenance and AI-assistance statements precede the bibliography.
The paper omits elementary quantum-algorithm tutorials, university front matter, long historical surveys,
inconsistent worked examples, and all implementation material. It retains the authoritative combiner and
formula-derived cost examples, and adds qualifications required to prevent unsupported conclusions. The
original numerical endpoint trace and every implementation-derived result are excluded. It uses a single
original diagram of old-tree delivery to a new root; no third-party figure is reproduced.

9 Decisions for the authors and supervisor
9.1 Scientific decisions before submission
1. Approve a complete byte-level profile for the thesis combiner: classical encapsulation interpretation,
exact KDF, encodings, labels, output lengths, associated data, nonce management, and rejection behavior.
The user's choice of the thesis resolves authority but does not fill unspecified parameters.
2. Define a formal state invariant for TreeCover key history, including removal, rejoining, new-member
mapping, retained-node eligibility, and retirement. Determine what evidence all receivers authenticate
before discarding old state.
3. Specify the adversarial and recovery model. Decide whether the target is passive post-quantum
confidentiality, active classical security, adaptive compromise, or some combination, and prove only the
selected properties under stated assumptions.
4. Resolve whether retirement remains purely cost-driven or additionally requires a freshness condition.
Define zero-denominator behavior, out-of-range ratios, target-set aggregation, and the equality case of the
new-tree threshold.
5. Provide a cumulative evaluation with exact topology and identical schedules for competing policies.
Include transition and retirement bytes. Test candidate arities beyond 2/4/8, or explicitly defend the
restriction. Keep analytical values and observations separate.
6. Obtain the full Bonsai paper and compare its algorithms directly with the final migration specification.
The present account uses verified abstract-level scope and cannot rule out closer overlap in the full work.

10 Skeptical review outcome
The central problem is precise and the paper now acknowledges the closest known higher-arity and
mKEM approaches. The design is coherent at the architectural level, while its cryptographic profile
remains incomplete. The security section is appropriately conditional and highlights a material recovery
issue. The analytical arithmetic is reproducible from the stated formulas. The endpoint trace is excluded
from the manuscript and provides no evidence for its claims. The abstract and conclusion are limited to
those findings.
The manuscript's main risk is scientific completeness, not English or Word formatting. A reviewer could
reasonably request a formal model, a complete implementation-independent specification, a stronger
novelty comparison, and reproducible cumulative evaluation before acceptance. The delivered paper
makes those limits visible rather than concealing them behind broad claims of quantum resistance,
efficiency, or preservation of post-compromise security.
